## 5 Reasons Your Feet Hurt After Playing Pickleball

### 1. Unforgiving Concrete and Asphalt Courts
Almost every outdoor pickleball court in Australia is painted directly over solid concrete or asphalt. Unlike lawn bowls greens or cushioned synthetic grass, hard acrylic courts have zero natural give, sending repeated impact shock waves straight into your heel bones and arch ligaments on every step, hop, and stop.

### 2. Wearing Running Shoes Instead of Court Shoes
This is the single most common equipment mistake new and social players make. Everyday running joggers feature soft, elevated foam heels and flexible mesh uppers engineered purely for straight-line forward strides.

When you push sideways to cut off a wide volley in a running shoe, your foot slides over the edge of the sole, your arch collapses inward, and your ankle risks rolling outward. Dedicated pickleball or tennis court shoes sit lower to the ground with reinforced side walls and firmer outsoles built for lateral braking.

### 3. Explosive Kitchen-Line Pivots and Backpedalling
Watch an experienced rally near the non-volley ("kitchen") line and you will see constant lateral crab-stepping, split-step hops on the balls of the feet, sudden forward lunges, and quick pivots to chase lobs (never backpedalling blindly if you can turn side-on!). Those multi-directional torques load small foot tendons from angles everyday walking never touches.

### 4. "Too Much, Too Soon" Enthusiasm
Going from occasional neighbourhood strolls to ninety minutes of court play three or four times a week outpaces how quickly mature tendons and foot bones remodel. Without gradual conditioning, tissues cross from healthy adaptation into micro-strain.

### 5. The Morning-After Inflammation Effect
During a match, warm muscles and lively match adrenaline mask early micro-irritation. Overnight while you sleep, natural inflammatory repair fluid gathers around overworked fascia and tendons, and your feet rest pointed slightly downward under the bedsheets. Stepping flat onto a cold floor in the morning stretches those stiffened tissues abruptly, producing classic next-day soreness.

## The 5 Most Common Pickleball Foot Injuries

1. **Plantar fasciitis:** Sharp, stabbing pain under the inner heel or arch during your first steps out of bed or after sitting in the car following a game.
2. **Achilles tendinitis:** Stiffness, thickening, or tender aching at the back of the heel and lower calf caused by repeated push-offs and split-steps.
3. **Lateral ankle sprains:** Rolling the ankle outward during a sharp side-cut or landing off-balance on an opponent's stray ball.
4. **Ball-of-foot pain (metatarsalgia and Morton's neuroma):** Burning, bruising, or tingling numbness beneath the forefoot from staying poised on your toes on hard courts.
5. **Foot stress fractures:** Tiny hairline bone cracks in the metatarsals or heel bone triggered by rapid jumps in weekly court hours without sufficient shock absorption or rest.

Left unchecked, every one of these local injuries also changes how you walk off the court, causing subtle limping that quickly tightens your hips and strains your lower spine. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### Why is wearing running shoes for pickleball risky for older players?
Running shoes have higher, softer heels built to cushion straight-ahead heel strikes, which makes them unstable during sudden side-to-side court lunges. Court shoes feature flatter, wider outsoles and reinforced side uppers that help prevent ankle rolls and arch strain.

### Why do my heels hurt the morning after pickleball when they felt fine during the game?
Warm circulation and endorphins mask minor micro-tears in the plantar fascia while you play. Overnight, your plantar tissue cools, tightens in a shortened position while you sleep, and becomes acutely sensitive during your first weight-bearing steps of the morning.

### Can orthotic insoles fit inside standard pickleball or tennis court shoes?
Yes. Almost all quality pickleball and tennis court shoes come with a removable factory insole. Lifting out that thin factory liner leaves clean room to slide in a full-length podiatrist-designed orthotic innersole without making the shoe tight.

### How long should post-pickleball foot soreness last before I see a podiatrist?
General foot muscle fatigue should settle within 24 to 48 hours. If sharp heel pain, Achilles stiffness, swelling, bruising, or forefoot burning lasts longer than a week or returns after every match, book a podiatry assessment promptly rather than playing through a developing injury.
